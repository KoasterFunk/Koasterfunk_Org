Mechanical Marvels

Ever since I can remember, I have always loved rollercoasters. I was a strange child though and I did not like riding them myself. The reason I loved them so much is the reason someone might enjoy Rube Goldberg machines. These fast and twisting train rides are mechanical marvels. We, as humans, somehow managed to create something that could have been so incredibly dangerous turn out to be safer than your car ride to get there.


As a child, it blew my mind that rollercoasters even worked. As an adult who has researched into their design process, I am blown away that they even exist. The engineering required to make these rides safe is underappreciated and genuinely my favorite thing about these glorified train rides. Now, however, I am much braver compared to my younger self. I not only enjoy watching rollercoasters, but I enjoy the thrill of riding them too.

RollerCoaster Tycoon

As I mentioned, I have loved rollercoasters since I was a child. That love evolved into me trying to find ways to scratch that itch when I was home and nowhere near an amusement park. Luckily for me, I had an old PC and my brother’s CD copy of a game called RollerCoaster Tycoon. This was my favorite game ever at the time, despite the fact I was really bad at it. I would play it all the time and just continue to build failure park after failure. I was having a blast!


One day though, a book fair at my school was selling CD copies of RollerCoaster Tycoon 3. A sequel to my favorite game ever being at school? Sure! I convinced my parents to get me the “educational” game and started playing it every chance I got.


To give the book fair credit, the game’s campaign mode would be considered educational as it involved management skills like the original games. The problem is that this game had a SANDBOX MODE. I exclusively played that mode when I found it and I could finally make a park that couldn’t fail because I had infinite money!


I was obsessed with this game -- I might even say I was addicted to it. And for that, many associate me with rollercoasters and still do to this day. I even took on the name “Koasterkid” when I first joined the internet. “Koaster” has stuck for many years now and I don’t think it will ever change.

My Upbringing

I believe I would not have become an engineer if I did not like rollercoasters. My love for creating started at the wonder of them, to games that involved them. I would then search for new games to perhaps create rollercoasters in, such as a game called Little Big Planet.


Little Big Planet is one of my favorite games ever as it taught me at a very young age that creativity is best when shared. Learning from others and sharing your mistakes is how you improve (It’s true!). When I started deconstructing someone’s rollercoaster to learn how it worked in that game, I went on the track to become an engineer. Even though I became an audio engineer, which is very different from a roller coaster engineer, my passion for rollercoasters still got to where I am.


My biggest passion is to create, and to me the rollercoaster is one of the greatest role models to have. They are the manifestation of thoughtful but crazy creativity constructed for others to enjoy. Even if you are scared to ride them like I was when I was a kid, you cannot deny they are extremely fascinating.


To someone like me, they were inspiring. They made me question if I could make something like that as well. Perhaps, I will create something that sparks inspiration in someone else one day. I believe if anyone puts in the work, they can make their own marvel.
