Dollywood Coasters

11/21/2026

Welcome to the second Funk this week! Instead of showing off something I’ve worked on, I have decided that I want to geek out about roller coasters. Particularly the roller coasters at Dollywood Tennessee!

There are currently 10 roller coasters at Dollywood, and I know a lot of information about them. I haven’t been to the park since the installation of 2 of the coasters, but the next time I go I do plan to get the credits.





I will briefly give my opinions about them all and a fun fact about each coaster, going in order of the areas they reside!



Wildwood Grove

Dragonflier:

This ride is a suspended family coaster manufactured by Vekoma. Suspended means that the track is above your head instead of below! Let me tell you, this coaster caught me off guard my first ride. It was a lot more intense than I was expecting, especially for a family coaster. It’s just a really good experience.


Big Bear Mountain:

This ride is a steel family launch coaster manufactured by Vekoma (they made a good impression!). This ride has three launches and built in speakers playing music and voice lines the whole ride. I have not been to the park since the ride was installed, so I have personally not been able to ride it. From the looks of it though, it looks like a really fun and easy ride!



Timber Canyon

Thunderhead:

This ride is a classic wooden coaster manufactured by Great Coasters International. Apparently, it was the first coaster ever to have a fly-through station! I think this is a fun ride with a surprisingly long run time. A really good ride that keeps you screaming!


Whistle Punk Chaser:

This ride is a steel junior coaster manufactured by Zamperla. It’s a very small coaster for kids, and I personally haven’t had the chance to ride it. I know I’m a grown man now, but I do want the credit anyway!


Mystery Mine:

This ride was the first “Euro-Fighter” model to be installed in the US by the German company Gerstlauer. It’s a steel coaster with small trains optimized for vertical lifts and drops. In fact, this ride has two vertical lift hills, with the biggest one being indoors!

This is one of my favorite rides there. It has amazing theming, is intense, and it has pyro effects before it drops you 85 feet.



Wilderness Pass

Wild Eagle:

This ride was the first wing coaster to be installed in the US by the Swiss company B&M. The trains have you sit out on the sides, being a very surreal experience. It has a 110 foot tall vertical loop and was personally my first “big boy” roller coaster. The ride itself is extremely smooth and not very forceful at all. The Dragonflier is more forceful than this ride, but it’s still a very VERY good experience and a lot more intense at a glance!


Fire Chaser:

This ride is a steel launched family coaster manufactured by Gerstlauer. Another surprisingly intense family ride that includes a BACKWARDS LAUNCH. I love the theming of this ride, and you can’t beat that unique launch.



Craftsman's Valley

Tennessee Tornado:

This was the last classic looping coaster to be built by the company Arrow Dynamics. It’s a shame because I would have loved to get more rides like this one. However, the ride designer, Alan Schlike, went on to work for Rocky Mountain Construction and design some of my favorite roller coasters in the world.

This ride is intense, sporting almost 4Gs during one of its inversions. If you don’t know, 4Gs means experiencing FOUR TIMES THE GRAVITY OF EARTH. I love this ride!


Blazing Fury:

This is a dark ride coaster manufactured by Herschend Creative. It’s a classic dark ride experience, with a 22-foot drop at the end. When I first rode this, I had no idea it was a roller coaster. It completely caught me off guard when It happened! I can’t forget that moment.



Jukebox Junction

Lighting Rod:

The only roller coaster in this area, and my favorite coaster PERIOD. This is a hybrid coaster manufactured by Rocky Mountain Construction, and it was the first roller coaster of its kind. It has a wooden structure with steel tracks, and it boasted an uphill launch. This launch was genuinely the best part of the ride, but sadly due to technical issues, they decided to replace it with a chain lift…

I have not rode this ride since this change, and I’m sad I may never get to experience this monstrous ride ever again.
